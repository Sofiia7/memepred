There is a meme coin on Robinhood Chain you follow, but no short prediction market for it.

FlipTheMeme gives you an UP or DOWN round on that coin, without buying it.

The current product is a Robinhood Chain testnet prototype. Players stake and collect test ETH.

Its three public demonstration pools have scripted prices. The public preview link is in the description.

The public desktop preview shows three listed pools and a direct ETH betting flow.

Each pool shows its depth and the largest matched bank a round can accept.

Both sides' totals are visible. Only equal stakes on UP and DOWN actually play.

Any excess comes back without a fee. If there is too little opposing stake, everyone gets a full refund.

A round first takes bets for five minutes, then waits through a five-minute pause.

The strike is averaged over the next minute. The exit is measured five minutes later.

Your bet is on the move from that future strike to the exit, not on the price when you click.

For example, a matched stake of 0.01 ETH would collect 0.0196 ETH if it wins.

The contract keeps two percent of the matched bank on a win. A tie or an unpriceable active round returns stakes minus one percent.

The trader confirms the future strike rule and places the bet in one wallet transaction. No swap or token approval.

After settlement, the player's ticket shows the result and a Collect button.

A winning ticket can be collected in ETH. This screen illustrates the result flow.

A development script completed 40 checks on the current testnet contract with the live keeper.

An UP stake of 0.005 WETH met an equal DOWN stake. The winner collected 0.0098 WETH.

The same run checked a tie and a one-sided refund. The script used WETH directly; the public interface uses ETH.

We also tested the round logic against a canonical pool on a local fork of Robinhood Chain mainnet.

The real pool had about 93.5 WETH of depth and enough price history to pass the listing gates.

At an unchanged price, the round tied. Then a simulated swap inside the fork moved the pool's price.

The contract settled UP and paid the winner. That swap was local simulation, not a mainnet trade.

Only matched trader stakes fund payouts. Pool depth limits how large a round can be.

The direct ETH path passed contract tests. A complete browser-wallet wager is still unrecorded.

This is an unaudited testnet prototype. Its development transactions do not prove user demand.
